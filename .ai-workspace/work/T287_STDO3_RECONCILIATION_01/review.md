## Concrete findings

### R1 — CatalogView cardinality contradicts the preserved Product domain

**Severity:** medium; blocks acceptance of the changed cross-view consistency
claim.

**Boundary:** computational binding §8 Domain view, line 282:
`DeclaredProgram "1" --> "0..*" CatalogView`.

**Counterexample:** a lawful narrowed view contains no Program; another lawful
view contains two Programs. The frozen M03 cardinality law permits zero Modules,
Programs, GraphFunctions or callable rows after narrowing, and its domain
diagram states `CatalogView "1" --> "0..*" GtlProgram`. Product F01/F09 and the
definition/tool/runtime split preserve that domain. The new relation instead
requires exactly one DeclaredProgram for each CatalogView. It is not labelled
as an invocation's selected Program projection; the separate ExecutionBasis
arrows already express that selection.

The new domain view therefore contradicts both the actual declared Catalog
domain and its other design projection. A competent implementer could narrow
Catalog validity to a single Program or reject the legal empty view. This
falsifies the changed view's cardinality-preservation claim under
DESIGN_MODULE §5 Decision-complete symbolic design and §7 cross-view law.

**Recommendation and bounded cost:** return this diagram relation to its
Writer for one local projection correction under existing Product/M03 law,
then reassess the exact changed relation. No Product change, runtime repair or
new proof campaign is needed.

### R2 — The result mapping omits distinctions needed for one closed value

**Severity:** medium; blocks acceptance of the changed result-interpretation
claim.

**Boundary:** computational binding §5, lines 120–122 and 129.

Two cases expose the missing partitions:

1. A reviewer has an exact, valid contract and authoritative expected basis,
   and evaluates a submitted request that contains a crossed or missing basis.
   The request violates a known admission constraint: line 120 yields
   `falsified` for “the subject is admissible.” Line 121 also matches that case
   and yields `invalid_basis`. The latter is appropriate when the evaluation's
   own subject/basis cannot support an evaluation, but the table does not
   distinguish those coordinates from the invalid submitted content being
   evaluated. A valid negative admission check must not lose its known
   counterexample merely because the tested request carries invalid basis data.
2. A required evaluator never runs because its declared actor/tool is
   unavailable, and no sufficient applicable evaluation exists without it.
   Line 122 assigns `indeterminate`; line 129 says an unperformed assessment
   has no result. These are different facts under FOUNDATIONS §4 Results,
   especially its “Out of frame or indeterminate” distinction. An actor that
   actually performs an evaluation but cannot decide from admissible evidence
   may return `indeterminate`; an unexercised required assessment instead stays
   a coverage gap with no closed result.

The general cause-preservation sentence does not disambiguate these explicit
table rows. Both cases are material to installed refusal proof, independent
assessment and qualification conjunction. The table currently calls itself
closed interpretation cases but admits incompatible outcomes for these inputs.

**Recommendation and bounded cost:** return this interpretation table to its
Writer to distinguish the evaluation basis from the submitted subject's
coordinates, and performed-but-undecidable evaluation from no performed
assessment. Reassess those finite cases against FOUNDATIONS Results and
SPEC_METHOD §11. No global runtime-result enumeration or runtime-union rewrite
is requested.
