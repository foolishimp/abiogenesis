## What STDO3 changes about the plan

The meaningful change is from a sequence of individually successful repairs to a declared construction from current state A to the unchanged Product destination Z. A includes physical work, exact governing bases, valid results, unfinished work and uncertainty about effects. Z includes the Product's acceptance conditions. A computational calculus constrains lawful transformations and compositions; it neither chooses Z nor proves that Z has been reached. This distinction is explicit in `ACTIVATION.md#construction-relation`.

For the requested thorough application, design must make the complete material relation set `M(B)` explicit at every affected boundary B: identity, cardinality, invariants, authorities and their transitions, functions, higher-order composition, topology, state/lifecycle, contracts, domains/admission, failure/refusal, effect/closure, projections, module mapping and material algorithmic obligations. `decision_complete(B)` requires no unresolved material relation, joint satisfiability, and semantic equivalence of all networks the design admits. A table of functions, valid schema, test count or plausible diagram does not establish that result. Retained implementation may not choose an unresolved durable semantic alternative. Conversely, already decision-complete internal refactoring does not require re-authoring unchanged design or repeating unaffected assurance. See `DESIGN_MODULE.md#proportional-design-sequencing` and `#decision-complete-symbolic-design`.

The computational projection must additionally show where each fact is established, every material consumer, exact basis/currentness and invalidators, shared computations, effects, retained alternatives and accumulated work. Domain-specific names do not prove different computational responsibilities. Similar machinery is only a commonisation candidate; identity, authority, lifecycle and failure differences must be evaluated before reuse or parameterisation. Each algorithmic obligation has both a semantic disposition (existing relation, extension, new candidate) and a realization disposition (consume, implement, optimise, adapt, deliberately duplicate with grounds). This applies directly to the 232–237 definition/history/native-payload work. See `DESIGN_MODULE.md#computational-realisation-projection-up-019`.

This is exhaustive design relative to a declared supported domain and complete material boundary. It is not proof of every possible program, model output, external condition or future defect. Unknowns must have declared handling. Whole-path evidence must conjoin applicable results without turning excluded or unperformed evaluations into successes. `ASSURANCE.md#significant-path-coverage`, `#computational-whole-path-evaluation`, and `ACTIVATION.md#4-totality-and-refinement` provide the relevant limits.
