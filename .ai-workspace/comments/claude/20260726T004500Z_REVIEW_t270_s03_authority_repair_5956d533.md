## F1 — Transformed, Not Closed (P1, blocking)

**The code is unchanged in substance.** The evaluator
(`developer-mini-product/src/index.ts:3358`) validates response contract,
`constructionIntentRef`, next-action basis ref/digest, and correction
disposition — but still performs **no** comparison of
`semanticEvidenceAssetRefs` against the pending intent's `outputAssetRefs`.
`isHumanApproval` accepts an empty array (`[].every(...)` is `true`;
`Set([]).size === 0`). The equality remains at `abg/continuation.ts:1207`,
i.e. `run.continue`. The negative test still asserts
`responded.disposition === "succeeded"` then
`completed.disposition === "refused"` — it moved from :2348 to :2527 with the
same assertions plus a duplicate-continue check.

**What did change is the design.** The invariant the prior review cited as
falsified — "Product semantics evaluates a response against the exact pending
request, `NextActionBasis`, and `ConstructionIntent`" — **no longer exists**.
Invariant 3 is now "ABG admits a continuation public operation before Product
response evaluation or resumed traversal." The design now states a different
position explicitly (M05:1725-1735):

> The F_H response is evidence input only. It cannot directly close the Run.
> … Expected output assets remain obligations and cannot be copied into
> evidence.

and lists "expected but unobserved output assets" as proven refusal #7 at the
continuation boundary.

**The design position has real merit** — I want to be fair to it. Validating
`semanticEvidenceAssetRefs === intent.outputAssetRefs` at respond would let
the caller pass by echoing the intent's own refs; the meaningful check is
against replay-visible admitted truth (`traversal_route.ts:2142` compares
against `actionRow.outputAssetRefs`). "Expected output assets remain
obligations and cannot be copied into evidence" is a deliberate anti-forgery
rule. And `responded.disposition === "succeeded"` arguably means "the
attributed response was recorded", not "the response is sufficient" — the
architecture demonstrably *can* refuse at respond when the Product choice is
wrong (`:3524`, correction-disposition mismatch, refused with
`target_mismatch` and **no** `fh_interaction_responded` event).

**Why it still blocks.** Three concrete contradictions in the current bytes:

1. **T-270 defect 2 is textually unchanged** (ticket line 110): "an F_H
   response that differs from the Product-owned pending choice can be recorded
   as succeeded before later continuation refuses to close." The ticket
   declares this a defect the correction **owns**; the checkpoint says
   "T-270's four defects bound what changed." The design now declares the same
   behavior lawful. Ticket and design are in direct conflict, and both are
   active authority.
2. **The finding was never dispositioned.** The checkpoint's "Review Findings
   Disposition" aggregates "the two reviews of candidate `48beb3f3`" — six
   items, none of which is the evidence-basis finding. From the durable record
   alone, F1 reads as *unaddressed*, not as *considered and rejected on
   design grounds*.
3. **An invariant that a review falsified was replaced rather than
   discharged.** Whatever the merits, "review falsifies invariant → invariant
   is rewritten" is the exact motion that produced the July 24/25 failures. It
   may well be correct here — but it must be *ruled*, not absorbed.

**What closes it.** An explicit F_H disposition, either:

- **uphold the design** — then reprice T-270 defect 2's text to state that
  response admission records attributed truth and evidence sufficiency is
  judged at basis derivation, record the supersession, and rename the test so
  it stops reading as the prohibited sequence; **or**
- **uphold the ticket** — then move the mismatch rejection to respond and
  assert refusal with no `fh_interaction_responded` event.

The first requires no code change and no new gate cycle.
